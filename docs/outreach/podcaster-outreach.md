## Why podcasters

A podcast episode is the cleanest input Flowstate has. A long conversation goes in,
and a canvas comes out — chapters on a spine, tangents branching below, and the quotes,
stats, claims and definitions pulled out as artifacts you can actually look at
(see [`transcript-artifacts`](../../.claude/skills/transcript-artifacts/SKILL.md)).

That makes podcasters two useful things at once:

1. **Testers** — they already have hours of transcript lying around, so they hit the
   real path on day one instead of a toy canvas.
2. **Distribution** — a canvas of their own episode is a show-notes page, a clip map,
   and a research artifact their audience wants. The demo *is* their content.

---

## How the phases work

The phase is decided by **the ask**, not by audience size.

| Phase | Who | The ask | What success looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Beta** | People reachable enough to get an hour of their attention. Small shows, or people already known personally. | "Use it on your next episode and tell me what broke." | Honest feedback, watched usage, bugs found before strangers find them. |
| **2 — Reach** | Established Indian shows with an audience worth borrowing. | "Here's a canvas of your episode — want it as show notes?" | A public artifact with their name on it; signups that come from their audience. |
| **3 — Scale** | Reference-grade international shows. | Nothing, until the product earns it. | Flowstate is good enough that a canvas of their episode is worth *them* sharing. |

Don't skip ahead. A phase-3 name reached too early is spent, not saved.

---

## Status vocabulary

Use exactly these, so the table can be scanned in one pass:

`Not started` → `Researching` → `Contacted` → `In conversation` → `Trying it` → `Active`

Terminal states: `Passed` (said no) · `Dormant` (no reply after two touches — revisit later, don't re-contact this cycle).

### Notes on the awkward ones

**Raj Shamani** — his YouTube About page links only Instagram, X, LinkedIn, Facebook and
Threads. No email, no website of his own. Third-party pages circulate
`partnerships@figuringout.co` and a contact form at `rajshamani.life/contact`; **neither is
linked from anything he controls**, the `.life` domain serves a page that reads as SEO
filler, and `figuringout.co` is an unrelated blog whose contact form still has a
`you@domain.com` placeholder in it. Treat that address as *unverified* — sending a pitch
there probably reaches nobody, and possibly reaches someone else. Use Instagram or LinkedIn.

**The BarberShop with Shantanu** — no email on the channel, the site, or the feed. Search
results that claim one serve it obfuscated behind a login. Instagram DM or the LinkedIn
showcase page are the honest routes. Shantanu Deshpande is also findable directly as the
Bombay Shaving Company founder, which is the stronger warm path.

**Rooted Growth Co** — genuinely no contact surface. 22 videos, started July 2025, no
Linktree, no bio links. A comment on a recent video is the only public route, and at 524
subscribers a comment will actually be seen.

**Rainmatter** — not a podcast and worth treating differently. It is Zerodha's VC arm; the
YouTube channel is its content output. The only contact route on the site is the
["Apply now" startup form](https://forms.gle/zg3QSsKpeBWqTgpy8), which is a funding
application, not a collaboration inbox. Also relevant: it is the institutional path to
Nikhil Kamath, already on the phase-2 list — decide which ask you are making before
touching either.

### The trick for every "none published"

YouTube's About page has a **"View email address"** button that reveals a channel's business
email after a captcha. It is behind a bot check, so it can't be pulled programmatically —
but it takes you ten seconds in a browser, and it is the most likely place to find addresses
for Raj Shamani, Shantanu, and Rooted. Worth doing for all three before falling back to DMs.

## Adding a name

Drop it in the phase that matches **the ask you'd make**, with whatever you know. A row
with just a name and a link is a valid row.

```
| Name | Show / @handle | reach or — | why them (one line) | route in | Not started | — |
```

Then, before contacting: fill `Why them` with something specific to *their* show (a named
episode, a recurring segment, a problem they've complained about on air). Generic reasons
get generic replies.
